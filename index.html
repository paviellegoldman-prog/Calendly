I came up through a big company — Allstate — where I learned early that every product decision is also a communications decision. The gap between what you build and what customers actually understand is always a positioning problem. That stuck with me.

At Artsy, I got to work at the sharpest edge of that problem: a two-sided marketplace where the seller's experience and the buyer's experience had to feel coherent, even though they were fundamentally different. I spent two years learning to hold those tensions — and to shape narratives that worked for both sides at once.

Calendly is a product people return to because it removes friction from how they work. My job is to make sure they know exactly why — and feel the value before they even log in.

Through my consulting practice, I've built GTM infrastructure where none existed — in environments with no playbook, high stakes, and founders who needed a strategic partner as much as an executor. That work sharpened my ability to bring structure to ambiguity, something called out explicitly in your JD and one of my favorite parts of the craft.

Outside of marketing, I facilitate writing workshops for caregivers — spaces where people reconnect with their voice and find language for what they already know. It's different work, same instinct: help people articulate what they already feel to be true. Good product marketing does exactly that.
